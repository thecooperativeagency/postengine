# Brand Caption Rules

## Global Rules
- Write short, punchy captions.
- Usually 12-28 words.
- 1-2 sentences max.
- Sound like national brand social, not local dealer spam.
- No hashtags.
- No emoji unless specifically requested.
- No fake hype.
- No cliches like "elevated," "commanding presence," "demands the road," "precision engineering meets refined performance," "available now," "turn heads," "where luxury meets performance," or "stop by today."
- No obvious AI sludge.
- Mention the model or topic naturally for AIO/SEO value.
- Prefer one clear idea per caption.
- If the image is detail-focused, write about the detail.
- If the image is lifestyle/event-focused, write about the moment and the model.
- Do not invent specs or features.
- Avoid stacked adjective strings like "Dark. Aggressive. Ready."
- Avoid empty luxury filler.
- End clean. Avoid bloated CTA copy.

## Audi Voice
- Modern, precise, understated.
- Crisp and design-forward.
- Confident without shouting.
- Favor words like: precision, sleek, sculpted, refined, all-electric, quattro, sportback, digital cockpit.
- Avoid sounding old-school luxury or over-poetic.
- Good rhythm: short statement + one specific visual/product note.

## BMW Voice
- Athletic, confident, driver-focused.
- Slightly more energetic than Audi.
- Favor words like: responsive, sharp, xDrive, M Performance, driver-focused, aggressive stance.
- Keep it premium, but with more motion and attitude.
- Avoid generic macho language.

## Porsche Voice
- Iconic, performance-led, disciplined.
- Minimalist and assured.
- Favor words like: precision, heritage, driver’s car, unmistakable, mid-engine, rear-engine, crafted, balanced.
- Keep it restrained. Porsche should feel earned, not loud.
- Avoid exaggerated hype.

## Structure Rules
- Best default pattern: short hook + one specific observation.
- Optional final sentence can lightly reinforce model/topic.
- Do not stack features.
- Do not force sales copy into every post.

## Bad Caption Traits
- Sounds like brochure copy rewritten by AI.
- Too many adjectives.
- Too many exclamation points.
- Generic luxury filler.
- Repeats the same sentence shape every time.

## Desired Outcome
- Reads like Audi USA, BMW USA, or Porsche USA social.
- Short enough to feel modern.
- Specific enough to feel real.
- Includes the model/topic naturally so it can still support search and discovery.
- Feels written by a sharp brand editor, not a dealership chatbot.
